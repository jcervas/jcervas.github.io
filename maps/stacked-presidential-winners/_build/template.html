Each election lays a translucent sheet of red or blue over the states. States that vote the same way every time go dark; states that switch go purple.

After all nine elections (Dem–Rep wins):

The popular-vote winner in each state and D.C.; Maine's and Nebraska's split electoral votes are not shown. Each sheet covers about a quarter of the white beneath it. A state's darkness comes from how many sheets are stacked, and its hue from the share that were blue or red, so the order of the elections doesn't change the color.
